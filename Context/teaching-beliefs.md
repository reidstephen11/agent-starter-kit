## Discipline-specific teaching

<!-- ASK NOW — the highest-value answer in this file. Generic AI output is weakest here.
     Q: "Forget general teaching strategy for a second. In your subject specifically, what
         does the teaching actually look like when it's going well? What's the move?"
     Eg: "Worked example first, every time, then fade the support. In practice: I code it on
         the board while they watch, then we do one together, then they do one alone. If I
         skip straight to 'have a go', the bottom third copies from a neighbour and learns
         nothing."
     Eg: "Source analysis. They don't get the argument until they've seen me interrogate a
         source out loud — who wrote it, when, what they wanted. Handing out a source
         analysis worksheet before I've modelled it produces summary, not analysis."
     Probe if the answer is a named strategy with no description of what it looks like in
     the room: ask "what does that look like in a lesson?" once, then move on. -->

## How prescriptive should a plan be?

<!-- ASK NOW — this is the single biggest control on over-production. Ask as two questions.
     Q1: "What must every plan you get from me specify — the things that aren't yours to
         decide in the moment?"
     Eg: "Curriculum alignment, the assessment it's building towards, success criteria, and
         the checkpoints where I find out whether they've got it. Safety, where it applies."
     Q2: "And what's yours — what should I leave alone?"
     Eg: "Activities, pacing, the context I hang it on, and anything I'd say out loud.
         I don't want scripted teacher talk and I don't want minute-by-minute timings."
     Probe once, if the second answer is vague: "Which annoys you more — a plan that's
     under-specified, or one that's over-specified?" Record the answer either way.
     Default if this heading is skipped: assume over-specification is the greater risk and
     say so in the handoff. -->

## The pedagogical approaches I use, and why

<!-- ASK LATER
     Q: "Are there named approaches or frameworks you actually use — and which parts of
         them do you use, as opposed to which parts you've been told about?"
     Eg: "Explicit instruction for anything procedural, and I use the I-do/we-do/you-do
         sequence properly. I use retrieval practice as a lesson starter. I've been through
         the inquiry PD but I only really use it for the Year 9 project term."
     Probe: ask how strong the teacher thinks the evidence is for each named approach, and
     record it. Cite with confidence proportional to what they say — not everything a
     teacher believes is equally well evidenced, and recording that keeps the agent honest. -->
